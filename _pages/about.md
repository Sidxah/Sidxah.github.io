I am a first-year Master's student in **Quantum & Distributed Computer Science** at [Université Paris-Saclay](https://www.universite-paris-saclay.fr/), and currently a research intern at [CEA LIST](https://list.cea.fr/) supervised by [Prof. Adrian Popescu](https://adrianpopescu.github.io/).

My work sits at the intersection of three areas that I think will define the next decade of AI research:

- **Self-supervised representation learning and world models.** How machines build internal models of reality from observation alone, in the spirit of Yann LeCun's [JEPA paradigm](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf), [V-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08471), and [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04489).
- **Quantum computing and Quantum Machine Learning.** Variational algorithms (VQE, QAOA), quantum error correction, and Quantum Reservoir Computing, where the boundary between classical and quantum advantage in learning is being actively redrawn.
- **High-performance and distributed systems.** The computational substrate that makes large-scale, world-model-class learning possible at all.

The thread connecting these is one question: **what computational primitives do we need to build systems that genuinely model their environment, rather than memorize patterns of it?**

## Research Vision

CLIP, SimCLR, DINO, MAE and I-JEPA showed that visual representations can be learned from weak supervision: from text, from masking, from contrast. The next step is harder. We need representations that **predict** rather than just describe, and that survive intervention and generalize to embodied, unseen settings, of the kind [DeepMind Genie 2](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-2-a-large-scale-foundation-world-model/) and [NVIDIA Cosmos](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/cosmos/) are reaching for.

I am drawn to two complementary questions in that direction:

1. **What does fine-grained textual supervision unlock in CLIP-style models?** This is the focus of my current TER at CEA LIST: whether richer, more specialized captions can yield representations that are not just zero-shot accurate but genuinely structured along the axes humans care about.

2. **Can quantum systems offer a non-trivial inductive bias for learning?** Recent work on [Quantum Reservoir Computing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13933) suggests that interference-based feature maps may capture correlations that classical random projections miss. I tested a small slice of this hypothesis at the [EPFL Quantum Hackathon](https://github.com/Sidxah/EPFL-Quantum-Hackathon-2026) on volatility-surface forecasting; verifying it more rigorously is something I want to keep working on.

I plan to pursue these questions through an M2 in AI research starting **September 2026**, then a PhD.
