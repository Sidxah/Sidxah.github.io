I am a first-year Master’s student in Quantum & Distributed Computer Science at Université Paris-Saclay, and currently a research intern at CEA LIST supervised by Prof. Adrian Popescu.

My work sits at the intersection of three areas that I think will define the next decade of AI research:

  • Self-supervised representation learning and world models. How machines build internal models of reality from observation alone, in the spirit of Yann LeCun’s JEPA paradigm, V-JEPA, and V-JEPA 2.
  • Quantum computing and Quantum Machine Learning. Variational algorithms (VQE, QAOA), quantum error correction, and Quantum Reservoir Computing, where the boundary between classical and quantum advantage in learning is being actively redrawn.
  • High-performance and distributed systems. The computational substrate that makes large-scale, world-model-class learning possible at all.

The thread connecting these is one question: what computational primitives do we need to build systems that genuinely model their environment, rather than memorize patterns of it?

Research Vision

CLIP, SimCLR, DINO, MAE and I-JEPA showed that visual representations can be learned from weak supervision: from text, from masking, from contrast. The next step is harder. We need representations that predict rather than just describe, and that survive intervention and generalize to embodied, unseen settings, of the kind DeepMind Genie 2 and NVIDIA Cosmos are reaching for.

I am drawn to two complementary questions in that direction:

  1. What does fine-grained textual supervision unlock in CLIP-style models? This is the focus of my current TER at CEA LIST: whether richer, more specialized captions can yield representations that are not just zero-shot accurate but genuinely structured along the axes humans care about.

  2. Can quantum systems offer a non-trivial inductive bias for learning? Recent work on Quantum Reservoir Computing suggests that interference-based feature maps may capture correlations that classical random projections miss. I tested a small slice of this hypothesis at the EPFL Quantum Hackathon on volatility-surface forecasting; verifying it more rigorously is something I want to keep working on.

I plan to pursue these questions through an M2 in AI research starting September 2026, then a PhD.
